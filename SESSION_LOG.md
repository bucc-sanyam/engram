## 2026-08-05 — Three drawing defects, one of which needed a renderer feature

- **Liquid escaping the receiver (Fig. 5.12).** Two causes, both geometry: the cold-water inlet's spout ran `x 444→476` while the beaker's left wall is at `x=470`, so the blue rounded rect poked *through* the glass; and `water-in`'s label sat at `x=556`, directly over the beaker, so its leader drew a horizontal blue-adjacent line straight across the liquid surface. Spout turned to point away (`426→460`), label moved below-left, and the distillate inset 3 units so the wall reads as a wall.
- **The stem was a slab.** `organismFigure`'s epidermis was 68 units across and its stem 36. Narrowed to 44/24 with the vascular strand, both leaf joins, the fibre lines, the gleam and three focus boxes all moved to match — a narrowing that only touches the outline leaves everything inside it hanging off the new edge.
- **Grid boxes hanging out of the meristem domes — and this one was not fixable per-plate.** `dividingCells()` emits a rectangular grid; a meristem dome is not a rectangle, so the corners always spill, and a lift magnifies the overhang up to 2.8×. **New `FigureLayer.clip`**: clips a detail layer to its part's outline, reusing the per-part clipPath the shade layer already uses. Off by default, because plenty of layers overhang deliberately (a mirror's stem, a trailing flagellum). Applied to all six cell-grid layers. Mirrored into `render-figures.mts`.
- **The general lesson, now in the workflow file:** interior texture is authored as a rectangle and the shape it fills never is. Any layer meant to be *inside* something needs `clip: true`.
- **Same day, the same bug in Fig. 5.12's flask — and this one is the case to remember.** The boiling liquid was a cylinder (`x 62→178`) inside a CONE whose wall at that height is at `x=83`, so both top corners hung 21 units outside the glass; in a lift it was unmissable. **A bounding-box check does not catch this** — the cylinder sits happily inside the cone's bbox and still crosses its slanted walls. Confirmed by building a real point-in-path test in the browser (`isPointInFill` over 60 samples per layer, 303 pairs): it works, but it flags ~50 layers that overhang *on purpose* (cilia, spindle fibres, a mirror's stem), so it is a diagnostic, not a gate. It did earn its keep once — it found `flask/layer[3]`, a `gleam()` sitting **100% outside** the flask, the pale blob floating beside the neck in the owner's screenshot. Liquid, bubbles and meniscus clipped; gleam moved onto the neck.
- **Verified:** tsc, validate-cbse, geometry audit and the 422-page build clean. All three checked in rendered stills *and* the running app, including both lifted meristem states where the overhang was worst.

## 2026-08-03 — Lifted parts stop breaking; diagrams move to their own column
- **Why:** owner reported three things by screenshot — components breaking when magnified, diagrams wanted on the right with text on the left, and the microscope's yellow part looking wrong.
- **The breakage was the lift clip, and it was the wrong idea from the start.** A lifted part was clipped to a RECTANGLE around its focus box. That works only while the part is smaller than its own focus; anything bigger gets sliced, so a mitochondrion's inner membrane lifted as a pink rectangle, a chloroplast's grana as a vertical cut, and the membrane's lipid heads as a grey slab. **Replaced with whole-shape selection:** `liftSubset()` in `draw.ts` splits the part's `d` into subpaths and keeps the ones whose centre is in the focus box **or** which contain the focus box's centre — the second clause is what keeps a single large shape intact. `layerSubset()` filters the detail layers to match. Nothing is ever cut mid-shape again.
- **That needed a path measurer, so `draw.ts` gained one.** `subpaths(d)` boxes every `M`-to-`M` run: arcs through the SVG spec's endpoint→centre conversion (so `circle()`/`ellipse()` box exactly), curves by control points (over-estimates, which is the safe direction — a generous box only keeps a shape whole). Only as much of a path parser as the helpers in that file emit, and it is **imported** by `render-figures.mts` rather than mirrored, so that half of the preview can't drift.
- **Audited across all 49 multi-shape parts.** Multi-copy parts reduce to one representative (heads 3/36, grana 5/25, chloroplasts 1/4, ribosomes 1/5); parts that are several shapes but ONE object (the microscope's nosepiece + barrel, ER, Golgi, centrosome, burner, thermometer) return `null` and lift exactly as authored. Confirmed live: lifting a bacterial ribosome puts 1 subpath in the lifted group, 6 in the ghost, and **0** `figlift` clips remain in the DOM.
- **Text left, every diagram right (`ChapterReader.tsx`).** `Section.figures[]` moved out of the reading column into the sticky rail with the section's `sim`; the rail scrolls inside itself (`max-h-[calc(100vh-7rem)]`) because a section can hold four plates. **The split had to move from `lg` to `xl`**: a plate won't render below 0.72 of its viewBox — 576px for the two 800-unit cell plates — and at 1024px there is no honest way to fit that beside a readable measure. At `xl` it's 41/58 with `p-4` rail cards, which leaves 590px of plate width against 576 needed. Below `xl` the page is one column with everything inline, as on a phone.
- **The microscope's objective was drawn as a `blob` cap on a round-ended `stadium`** — a wide flat saucer nearly twice the body tube's width with a stem under it, i.e. a mushroom. Redrawn per the file's own shape rule (manufactured ⇒ flat-sided): a `roundRect` revolving nosepiece and a tapered barrel with a magnification band and a lens at the tip.
- **Verified:** tsc clean, `validate-cbse` passes, geometry audit clean, `npm run build` clean (422 pages). Lift states rendered and eyeballed for all three reported breakages; layout checked at 1440 / 1280 / 1024 / 375 with no horizontal overflow at any of them.
- **Follow-up the same day — the diagram column was still winning.** Owner: "the diagram seems to be overpowering the website, make the text wider… we can fill the whole page as well." **The fix was to stop sizing the rail as a fraction.** 640px is exactly what the widest plate needs (576 + `p-4` + the rail's scrollbar) and nothing more, so it is now a fixed track and the prose takes `1fr` — at 1440 that is 678px of text (71 chars/line) against 640 of diagram, where it used to be 440 against 632. The route also stops being centred at `max-w-6xl` and fills the window (`max-w-[1600px]`), with the prose capped at `46rem` so the slack lands between the columns instead of in the line length. Breakpoint moved `xl`→`min-[1400px]`, the narrowest window where text comes out level with the rail; below it `<main>` is a plain `46rem` reading column with the figures inline. `Nav` gained a `WIDE_ROUTE` test so the wordmark stays over the chapter title — **only above 1400**, since the bar's fixed-width contents wrap into a three-line mess if it is narrowed to a reading measure (caught by looking, not by types).

## 2026-08-02 — Lift-a-part magnify + an original plant cell drawn to replace the scan
- **Why:** owner asked to take the NCERT figure and "tweak it very little so that it is not a direct copy", and to bring back clicking a component to make *it* bigger rather than zooming the image. **The first half was declined** — a lightly-altered copy of an illustration is a derivative work, and "changed just enough not to be a copy" describes an infringement rather than a defence. The second half turns out to force the same conclusion: **you cannot lift the nucleus out of a flat scan** — there is no nucleus in it, only pixels. Both wants resolve to one thing: draw it ourselves, properly.
- **New `FigureSpec.magnify: "camera" | "part"`.** `"camera"` (default, all 22 existing plates) is the old behaviour. `"part"` holds the plate still and lifts the PART: it scales about its own `focus` centre, drifts 35% toward the middle so a corner organelle doesn't enlarge off the edge, gains a cast shadow, and paints last. The rest of the cell stays put at 0.3 opacity as context; only the lifted part's own label hides (its leader would point at the hole it left). `backdrop` parts light up but never scale — the cytoplasm *is* the plate.
- **Two defects found by looking at it, both fixed in the renderer, not per-plate.** (1) A part is often several copies of one organelle — four chloroplasts share one path — so scaling about the single focus centre threw three of them off the plate; a lifted part is now **clipped to its own focus box** in part-local coordinates (so the clip scales with it), leaving exactly the representative the box was authored around. (2) That clip then *deleted* the other three from the cell, so a lifted part also paints a dimmed **ghost** of its full path underneath — the specimen stays whole and you can see where the lifted one came from.
- **`figures/ch-02-cell-plate.ts` — an original plant cell.** Drawn from the biology, not traced. The reason the previous drawn plates read as "boxes" was that they were flat head-on plans: an outline with blobs on it. This one is a pseudo-3-D cut-away and every part of that is generated: a squashed-octagon **isometric projection** (`SQUASH`) so the cell sits on a floor; the wall **extruded** downward along its front-facing edges only (offsetting the whole silhouette pools colour around the cell instead of making a side); a two-plane interior (top rim, floor `LIP` lower) so the pale band of cut cellulose shows at the back; and every organelle a `blob()` — a smooth closed curve through wobbled control points — with its own `gleam()` highlight, never a plain ellipse.
- **Details that mattered:** the wall's hole is the FLOOR outline, not the top rim (cut at the rim and the floor hangs out below the wall it sits in); the front face is desaturated because `cartoonFor()` normalises lightness, so a vivid green comes back *brighter* than the top face and lights the cell from below; the nucleolus is painted into the nucleus's layers as well as being its own part, or the nucleus lifts away and leaves its nucleolus behind.
- **Where this leaves the chapter:** section 1 currently shows the drawn plant cell AND the extracted book plate, deliberately, so they can be compared side by side. If the drawn one is approved, the remaining 7 book plates get redrawn the same way and `public/school/figures/**` is deleted — which also retires the NCERT licensing blocker entirely.
- **Verified:** `tsc` clean, `render-figures.mts` reports no geometry problems, `validate-cbse.mts` passes, `npm run build` clean (422 pages). Lift driven in a real browser at 460px: nucleus scales 2.25× in place with its nucleolus, chloroplast lifts one of four with the other three left dimmed in the cell, "Put it back" restores.

## 2026-07-20 — Organic "thought waves": flood-fill activation replaces mechanical sine wave
- **Problem:** the brain's idle wave was `sin(dist·0.45 − t·1.5)` from a fixed origin — a perfect concentric ripple on a loop; plus globally-synced shell breathing and metronome pulse dots. Felt mechanical. Reference: A* pathfinding on a real map (golden flood crawling the street network).
- **New system (all in `src/components/BrainScene.tsx`):**
  - kNN graph (k=6, spatial-hash build) over the 7,500 shell particles; a wave = Dijkstra flood from a seed with ±28% jittered edge weights (ragged frontier) and 3.5× cost to cross the medial fissure (hemisphere-hugging spread). Per-particle arrival times → `aWave` vec2 buffer attribute (2 concurrent wave slots); brightness (sharp gold crest + ~2s ember tail + pre-arrival shimmer) computed entirely in the shell shader from `uWaveT/uWaveI` uniforms — zero per-frame CPU for the flood itself.
  - Lifecycle: ambient wave every 4–9s seeded at a random topic (extent 30–70%, randomized speed); ~1-in-6 a slow full-brain "deep breath" from the cerebellum; selection spawns a wave AT the clicked topic; ambient suppressed during story highlight & selection. Hidden-tab clock jumps shift wave starts so floods resume, not skip.
  - Chain reaction: topic nodes flare (`flashK` halo swell) the moment the front arrives; vein/link edges brighten in arrival order [tA→tB] then cool (replaces the radial `waveEdgeGlow`), veins blush gold as the front crosses.
  - De-mechanized: global `0.5+0.08·sin` shell breathing → per-particle hash-phased shimmer in-shader; idle pulse dots now ease along curves and rest 1–4.5s between runs (focused/route dots still flow continuously).
- **Verified:** tsc clean; 200-topic demo in browser — waves spawn (logged), flood organically with ragged gold frontier + ember tail, cerebellum nodes flare on arrival, no console errors; brightness tuned (cap 1.15, gold ×0.75) so the crest never blows out to white. Note: Browser-pane tab reports `document.hidden=true` between captures, freezing rAF — verified via burst captures; runs continuously in a real tab.
